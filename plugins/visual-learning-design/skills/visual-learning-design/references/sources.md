# Sources

Primary and secondary sources behind this skill, with what each supports. Cite these when the user asks for evidence. Effect sizes in this skill come from Mayer (2021) as summarized in Mayer (2023) unless noted otherwise.

## Core framework

- **Mayer, R. E. (2021).** *Multimedia Learning* (3rd ed.). Cambridge University Press. 15 principles grouped by extraneous, essential, and generative processing, with meta-analytic medians.
- **Mayer, R. E. (2023).** Research-based principles for designing multimedia instruction. In Overson, Hakala, Kordonowy & Benassi (Eds.), *In Their Own Words*. APA Division 2. Source of the effect-size table, the boundary conditions, and the solar-cell slideshow worked example. https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-research-based-principles-for-designing-multimedia-instruction-mayer.pdf
- **Mayer, R. E., & Fiorella, L. (Eds.). (2022).** *The Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning* (3rd ed.). The extended list of about 30 principles: expertise reversal, multiple representations, transient information, and others.
- **Clark, R. C., & Mayer, R. E. (2023).** *e-Learning and the Science of Instruction* (5th ed.). Wiley. Worked examples, practice, feedback, learner control, and games in e-learning.
- **Digital Learning Institute.** "Mayer's Principles of Multimedia Learning." The seed article, covering the classic 12 principles with practitioner applications. https://www.digitallearninginstitute.com/blog/mayers-principles-multimedia-learning

## Specific principles

- **Coherence / seductive details:** Mayer, Heiser & Lonn (2001); Moreno & Mayer (2000), background music and sounds; Mayer, Griffith, Jurkowitz & Rothman (2008); Rey (2012), meta-analysis; Sundararajan & Adesope (2020), "Keep it coherent," *Educational Psychology Review* 32, 707–734.
- **Signaling:** Mautone & Mayer (2001); Richter, Scheiter & Eitel (2016) and Schneider, Beege, Nebel & Rey (2018), both meta-analyses.
- **Redundancy:** Moreno & Mayer (2002); Adesope & Nesbit (2012), meta-analysis; Kalyuga & Sweller (2022), Handbook chapter.
- **Spatial and temporal contiguity:** Moreno & Mayer (1999); Mayer & Anderson (1991); Ginns (2006) and Schroeder & Cenkci (2018), both meta-analyses.
- **Segmenting:** Mayer & Chandler (2001).
- **Pre-training:** Mayer, Mathias & Wetzell (2002).
- **Modality:** Ginns (2005), meta-analysis; Reinwein (2012); Castro-Alonso & Sweller (2022).
- **Personalization:** Mayer, Fennell, Farmer & Campbell (2004); Ginns, Martin & Marsh (2013), meta-analysis.
- **Voice:** Mayer, Sobko & Mautone (2003); Craig & Schroeder (2017), *Computers & Education* 114, and (2018), *J. Educational Computing Research* 57. Modern TTS is roughly comparable to a recorded human voice.
- **Embodiment:** Mayer & DaPra (2012); Fiorella & Mayer (2016), instructor drawing; Fiorella, van Gog, Hoogerheide & Mayer (2017), first-person video; Davis (2018), gesture meta-analysis.
- **Image:** Mayer, Dow & Mayer (2003); Schroeder & Adesope (2014).
- **Immersion:** Makransky, Terkildsen & Mayer (2019), more presence but less learning; Parong & Mayer (2018), summarizing in VR.
- **Generative activity:** Fiorella & Mayer (2015), *Learning as a Generative Activity*, Cambridge University Press. Source of the activity-level effect sizes. Also Wittrock (1989).

## Related effects

- **Expertise reversal:** Kalyuga (2007), *Educational Psychology Review* 19, 509–539; Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler & Sweller (2003).
- **Worked examples and fading:** Sweller, Ayres & Kalyuga (2011), *Cognitive Load Theory*; Salden, Aleven, Schwonke & Renkl (2010), adaptive fading.
- **Subgoal labels:** Catrambone (1998); Margulieux, Guzdial & Catrambone (2012).
- **Animation:** Höffler & Leutner (2007), meta-analysis, *d* = 0.37; Berney & Bétrancourt (2016), *g* = 0.23; Tversky, Morrison & Bétrancourt (2002), "Animation: can it facilitate?", the congruence and apprehension principles.
- **Graphic functions:** Clark & Lyons (2011), *Graphics for Learning* (2nd ed.), Wiley.
- **Implicit scaffolding in simulations:** Podolefsky, Moore & Perkins (2013), arXiv:1306.6544 (PhET).

## Medium-specific

- **Video:** Brame (2016), "Effective educational videos," *CBE—Life Sciences Education* 15(4), es6, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5132380; Guo, Kim & Rubin (2014), "How video production affects student engagement," *ACM Learning@Scale*; Szpunar, Khan & Schacter (2013), interpolated testing; Noetel et al. (2021), "Video improves learning in higher education," *Review of Educational Research*: *g* = 0.28 when replacing existing teaching, *g* = 0.80 when added to it.
- **Slides:** Alley & Neeley (2005), "Rethinking the design of presentation slides," *Technical Communication*; Garner & Alley (2013), *International Journal of Engineering Education* 29(6), assertion-evidence comprehension study; Issa et al. (2013), *Medical Education* 47, a medical lecture redesigned using Mayer's principles.
- **Short-form and social:** Zhang, Lucas, Bem-Haja & Pedro (2023), "Analysis of short videos on TikTok for learning Portuguese as a foreign language," *Comunicar* 77. In 34 videos, coherence was the principle applied least (29%) and signaling the most (100%). The number of generative-processing principles a video used correlated with likes (r = .36). Small sample, correlational, and likes are not learning. Verizon Media & Publicis Media (2019) survey: about 69% of US consumers watch video sound-off in public. The often-quoted 85% and 92% figures are platform-specific (Facebook) or mobile-specific. Sanchez, Booth & Karolczak (2024/25): learners value relatability, humor, and authenticity in short-form educational video.
- **Games:** Mayer (2014), *Computer Games for Learning*, MIT Press.
- **Accessibility:** W3C WCAG 2.2: 1.1.1, 1.2.2, 1.2.5, 1.4.3, 1.4.11, 2.2.2, 2.3.1, 2.3.3.

## Caveats to pass on

- Most of the evidence comes from short lab studies with college students learning how a system works, measured on immediate transfer. Effects in long, authentic courses are generally smaller but point the same way.
- The redundancy effect is small (*d* = 0.10), and reverses in several conditions. It never justifies withholding captions.
- Some popular statistics (for example, "slides improve retention by 32%") lack a clear primary source. Don't repeat them.
